What to expect in each round
1

Recruiter Screen (30 min)

Career background, tools, team size, comp expectations. Mention H-1B early.

💡 Have your TC ask ready. Know your target: $160K+ base or total comp?
2

Technical Phone Screen (45–60 min)

1–2 medium LeetCode problems. Focus on arrays, trees, hashmaps. Code in Python. Talk through every step.

💡 Name your assumptions and catch your own bugs before the interviewer does.
3

ML Concepts Round (45 min)

Core theory: transformers, gradient descent, regularization, evaluation metrics. Not just definitions — explain trade-offs and business impact.

💡 "It reduces dimensionality" is a failing answer for PCA. Go deeper.
4

ML System Design (60 min)

Design an end-to-end ML system. RAG systems, recommenders, inference batching pipelines, and LLM-powered agents are most common in 2026. Start with business objective, then data → model → eval → deploy → monitor.

💡 Anthropic asks about inference batching for a single GPU with 100 concurrent requests. Prep this specifically.
5

AI-Assisted Coding (60 min) — NEW

Debug or extend a codebase using an AI assistant in the IDE. They're watching how well you direct the AI, catch mistakes, and override it when needed.

💡 Practice with Cursor or Copilot. The signal is judgment, not speed.
6

Behavioral / Values (45 min)

Use STAR format. Quantify outcomes. At safety-focused companies (Anthropic, OpenAI), expect: "Tell me about a time you made a safety-first decision" or "a time you built something that conflicted with your values."

💡 "Cut p95 latency from 4s to 900ms" beats "it got faster." Always put a number on it.
7

Take-Home (varies)

Common at AI startups. Build a RAG feature, a memory engine, or an eval harness. Keep to under 4 hours. If it feels like free consulting (72-hour projects), it's okay to withdraw.

💡 Anthropic's take-home: code optimization for speed, 4-hour Python workload on TPU-like ops.
